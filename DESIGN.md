# Keepsake design direction

## Product metaphor

Keepsake is a private correspondence archive: part address book, part index-card file, part notebook. It should feel kept and handled, not generated or optimized.

## Visual rules

- Prefer paper, rules, tabs, stamps, and annotations over generic floating cards.
- Use Libre Caslon Display for human moments, IBM Plex Sans for utility, and IBM Plex Mono for dates and filing labels.
- Keep the palette material and restrained: paper, ink, forest, brick, and blue pencil.
- Use square edges, hairline borders, and offset print-like shadows. Avoid glass, glow, gradients, and universal pill shapes.
- A component earns decoration only when it reinforces the archive metaphor.
- Show provenance whenever possible: who a memory belongs to and which note produced it.
- Copy should be direct and specific. Avoid generic encouragement, “AI-powered,” and “magic” language.
- Preserve familiar navigation and large tap targets even when the visual treatment is editorial.

## Quality bar

Every screen must have a clear information hierarchy, usable empty/loading/error states, keyboard-visible focus, and a reason for every card or visual accent. The interface should still be recognizable as Keepsake with the logo removed.
